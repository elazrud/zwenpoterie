I
Chapter I 1975–1986 • Old Medina of Safi

The Boy from Safi

If you want to know how ZWEN POTERIE started, you have to go back to the old medina of Safi, where our father and founder, El Mostafa EL AZROUD, was born in 1975 in the Dar Bouaouda neighborhood. Almost everyone in his family made a living with their hands—his dad was a baker, his older brother made pottery, and his uncle worked in crafts, too.

In 1986, at 11 years old, everything clicked. While spending his school holidays at his older brother’s workspace, he touched wet clay for the very first time. He still talks about how smooth and yielding it felt in his hands that day. That single moment decided his whole future.

“The clay does not care who you are until your hands become calm. In that quiet moment in 1986, my path was chosen.” — Master El Mostafa on His First Touch of Clay
II
Chapter II Apprenticeship • The Potter's Wheel

Learning the Ropes (and the Clay)

He didn’t just get to sit down at the potter’s wheel and start throwing. His brother was his first maalem (master artisan), and he made him earn it. For a whole year, our dad was tasked with the heavy, exhausting work of gathering, preparing, and wedging the tough clay. It was grueling physical labor, but quitting never crossed his mind. Finally, he was allowed to shape his own pieces from start to finish: a little vase and a pen holder.

That apprenticeship taught him the most important rule of authentic Moroccan ceramics: you have to deeply respect what you make with your own hands. Eventually, he left home to learn more, traveling and working alongside other artisans in Marrakech, Tunisia, and Libya before bringing all that experience back with him.

“Before you are worthy of shaping the clay, you must carry it, wedge it, and understand its weight. True craft cannot be rushed.” — The Rule of the Maalem
III
Chapter III 2003 • From Safi to Agadir

Building Our Agadir Pottery Workshop

By 2003, after years of honing his craft, he was ready to strike out on his own. He set up our Agadir pottery workshop because he loved the city's energy and wanted to share our culture with people visiting from all over the world. It wasn't easy at first. He actually had to build his very first kiln with the help of a friend. The real turning point came when another friend working at a local hotel helped him land a massive order for custom plates and cups. Delivering that successfully gave him the financial footing to finally stand on his own as an independent maker.

When we named the business, we chose ZWEN POTERIE. “Zwen” means beautiful in Moroccan Darija, and “Poterie” is French for pottery. It’s a mix of languages that perfectly describes what we do: make beautiful things that bring a warm, welcoming energy to your home.

“When someone welcomes a piece of handcrafted pottery into their home, they are inviting forty years of Moroccan memory and warmth.” — The Meaning of ZWEN POTERIE
IV
Chapter IV Local Red Clay • Secret Glaze Formulas

Local Red Clay & Secret Colors

If you walk into our workshop today, we want you to feel like you just stepped into our living room. Everything we make relies on local red clay. We source it right here in the region because the quality is incredible and it keeps our production 100% local.

Even though our style has evolved to include modern shapes, our dad absolutely refuses to let go of traditional hand-coiling techniques. He’s still happiest when he’s throwing large vases on the wheel. To finish the pieces of Moroccan handmade pottery we craft, we strictly use high-standard, food-safe commercial glazes. But over the years, he’s developed his own secret mixing recipes—which is how we get those unique, mesmerizing colors you won't find anywhere else.

“We don't borrow industrial recipes. The soul of our glaze is born from our own earth, minerals, and fire.” — Authentic Moroccan Mastery
V
Chapter V Cooperative Legacy • The School Ahead

An Artisan Cooperative Today

Working the clay for decades takes a real physical toll on the body. But for our father, sitting in the quiet spin of the wheel is like meditation. It taught him that happiness comes from patience.

Today, seeing his family and artisans working together as an authentic Moroccan artisan cooperative to develop this craft and preserve this heritage is his proudest achievement. As a cooperative, we ensure that every piece carries the human touch, pure natural materials, and cultural history that industrial factories cannot replicate. Looking ahead, our dad’s biggest dream isn’t to build an empire. He wants to turn the cooperative's workshop into a dedicated school to teach the next generation of artisans how to keep this craft alive.

“The greatest legacy is not building an empire, but passing the wheel to hands that will keep the Moroccan flame alive.” — The Philosophy of Legacy
The Eternal Whisper

If he could go back in time and whisper one piece of advice to that 11-year-old kid covered in wet clay, he wouldn't warn him about the hard work ahead. He’d just say:

“Smile, life is a short story.”
— Master El Mostafa El Azroud
